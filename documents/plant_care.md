
# Plant Care Guide

## Watering
Water a plant according to its species, pot size, soil type, and growing conditions. Check the soil before watering. Many common houseplants benefit from allowing the top layer of soil to dry between waterings. Avoid leaving roots standing in water.

## Light
Different plants need different light levels. Some thrive in bright indirect light, while others tolerate lower light. Observe the plant for signs of insufficient or excessive light, and adjust its position gradually.

## Soil and Drainage
Use a pot with drainage holes. Choose a growing medium appropriate for the plant species. Poor drainage and persistently wet soil can contribute to root problems.

## Fertilizer
Follow the fertilizer label and the plant's needs. Avoid excessive fertilizer, which can damage roots. Many houseplants need less fertilizer during periods of slow growth.

## Yellow Leaves
Yellow leaves can have several causes, including overwatering, underwatering, insufficient light, nutrient issues, or natural aging. Check the soil moisture, drainage, light, and recent care history before deciding on a treatment.

## Pests
Inspect leaves, stems, and the undersides of leaves for pests. Identify the pest before selecting a treatment. Follow product instructions and keep treatments away from children and pets as directed by the label.

## Repotting
Repot a plant when its roots become crowded, the growing medium breaks down, or water no longer drains properly. Choose a pot with drainage holes that is only moderately larger than the current pot. Avoid unnecessary repotting when a plant is stressed.

## Humidity and Temperature
Different plant species have different humidity and temperature requirements. Keep plants away from sudden temperature changes and cold drafts. Check species-specific needs before changing the plant's location.

## Pruning
Remove dead or damaged leaves with clean tools. Prune according to the plant species and avoid removing too much healthy growth at once. Clean tools between plants to reduce the risk of spreading disease.
